                            Assignment 3: Spec-Driven Development
Software engineering (SE) practice is transforming to use SE agents that take requirements as inputs,
called “specs,” and generate code as output. This permits developers to focus more on writing the right
requirements and on quality assurance than on writing code. In this assignment, students will practice
spec-driven development using GitHub Spec Kit and an AI-assisted development tool. Students will use
stakeholder interviews and goal models to identify and prioritize requirements, develop specifications,
build prototypes, and evaluate how well the resulting implementation supports stakeholder goals.

The objective is not simply to build a working application, but to understand how human judgment
contributes to requirements selection, conflict resolution, traceability, and specification refinement,
when AI can generate most of the implementation.

Required Readings
•​ GitHub Spec Kit QuickStart1 and Command Reference2

Learning Objectives
•​ Guide SE agents in translating stakeholder requirements into code
•​ Trace and evaluate whether code satisfies requirements and discover new requirements through
   implementation
•​ Identify and distinguish complementary roles for human developers and SE agents in software
   design

Assignment Deliverables​
You are a software engineer assigned to a team at ABC Design Services, who is responsible for elicitation,
requirements modeling and analysis, sprint planning, and requirements verification. Your team has
recently completed goal modeling from stakeholder interviews. Your task is to review the goal models
and plan two prototypes, after which you will supervise an SE agent in the implementation of the
prototypes to ensure the software satisfies the associated requirements. Your employer is very optimistic
that SE agents will replace human developers, so your task is to carefully monitor your agent(s) and
ensure they are doing good work, while also documenting your role throughout the process.

Your job is to be faithful to stakeholder needs and expectations, anticipate additional needs and risks,
and validate that “additional needs” are necessary or desired.

Because your company does not use a formal process (e.g., Scrum), you have wide latitude to decide
what portion of the requirements that you have collected will be included in your first deliverable. You
should choose goals that demonstrate a core feature, a user-facing aspect or an aspect with high
uncertainty and clearly justify your choice. This selection will be used in a mock sprint to develop this
feature or aspect.

Step 1: Review the goal models that your team members created in Assignment #2. Based on the three
priority criteria (core, user-facing, uncertain), choose a subset of the goals from your stakeholders to
become the system boundary for your prototypes. Merge and reconcile any differences between your
team members' goal models: one team member may have identified more detail than another on a
particular aspect of the system, or this detail may be distributed across multiple team members.
Combine your findings to realize the best model for this assignment. Justify your decision by considering
other priorities that you decided were less important and include those in your learning log, below.

Step 2: Review the relationships between the selected requirements and stakeholder goals. Identify any
meaningful overlap, dependency, or conflict involving your selected requirements. For example, two
stakeholders may request different behaviors for the same feature, or two requirements may support
the same goal but propose different approaches. Use your goal model to explain the relationship and
justify your decision. Where requirements overlap, determine whether they should be combined,
implemented separately, or prioritized differently. Where goals conflict, explain the trade-off and how
you decided to proceed. You may prioritize one goal, develop a compromise, or document an unresolved
conflict that requires additional stakeholder clarification. Do not simply remove conflicting goals or
requirements from the model. Preserve the relationship and document the reasoning behind your
decision. Update your goal model to reflect your sprint scope and any decisions or assumptions made.

Step 3: Create your development environment by creating a GitHub private repository GitHub Spec Kit
and an AI-assisted development tool, translate your selected requirements into a “spec”. Your initial spec
should include:

    ●​   The selected stakeholder goals and corresponding requirements.
    ●​   Acceptance scenarios defining how the requirements can be evaluated.
    ●​   Relevant assumptions, dependencies, and edge cases.
    ●​   Decisions made regarding overlapping or conflicting requirements.

Use Spec Kit to generate an implementation plan and development tasks, then build a functional
prototype based on the specification. You should use mock data and simplified interactions where
appropriate. The prototype should only demonstrate the selected requirements rather than attempt to
implement all elicited goals. Preserve your initial specification, goal model, Spec Kit artifacts, and
prototype source code as Version 1 (V1) in GitHub. Deploy Prototype V1 and retain its URL.

Step 4: Interact with Prototype V1 and evaluate whether it satisfies the requirements you selected for
the sprint. Review and assure you have traceability between the interview transcripts, goal model,
specification, and implementation. For each selected requirement, identify:

    ●​ The corresponding goal in your goal model.

Step 5: Use the findings from your evaluation to improve the specification. Revisit your original
requirements decisions, including any conflicts or overlaps identified earlier. Update the goal model
where your understanding of goal relationships or satisfaction has changed. Preserve unresolved
stakeholder conflicts and document any assumptions or trade-offs. Revise the specification before
changing the intended implementation behavior. Use the revised specification to update the relevant
planning artifacts and prototype. Preserve the revised specification, goal model, and implementation as
Version 2 (V2) in the same GitHub repository. Deploy Prototype V2 separately so that both versions can
be accessed and compared. Your revised version should demonstrate at least one meaningful
requirements-level improvement informed by evaluating Prototype V1.

Step 6: Prepare a single document named learning-log.md describing your decisions, observations, and
learning throughout the sprint.

The document should contain the following sections.

1. Sprint Planning and Requirements Selection

Briefly describe the requirements and the goals you selected for the sprint, and why you prioritized
them. Identify the overlapping, dependent, or conflicting requirements you considered. Briefly explain
how your goal model helped you understand these relationships, what decisions you made, and why.

2. Prototype Evaluation

Discuss your significant observations from Prototype V1. Explain whether the issues arose from the
specification or implementation and what they revealed about the stakeholder goals.

3. Revisions and Learning

Describe the significant changes between Versions 1 and 2, including changes to the goal model,
specification, and prototype. For each significant change, explain:

    ●​   What you initially intended.
    ●​   What you discovered through evaluation.
    ●​   What you changed and why.
    ●​   How the revised implementation better addresses the requirement or stakeholder goal.

Conclude with a brief reflection addressing:

    ●​ Which decisions required your judgment rather than relying solely on AI?
    ●​ How did prototyping change your understanding of stakeholder needs?
    ●​ How did spec-driven development help maintain consistency between the requirements and
       implementation?
    ●​ What evidence from your work would you present to management to demonstrate the value
       you contributed beyond AI-generated specifications and code?

The learning log should emphasize your reasoning and decisions rather than simply listing the commands
or prompts used during development.

Evaluation Criteria​
Your work will be evaluated on your ability to:
   1.​ Select and justify requirements: Identify a coherent sprint scope grounded in stakeholder
       evidence and goal models.
   2.​ Analyze goal relationships: Recognize overlapping, dependent, and conflicting requirements and
       justify the decisions made.
   3.​ Apply spec-driven development: Produce coherent specifications, planning artifacts, and
       working prototypes.
   4.​ Establish traceability: Connect stakeholder interview evidence to goals, requirements, and
       implemented functionality.
   5.​ Evaluate and revise: Identify meaningful requirements issues, improve the specification and goal
       model, and demonstrate the resulting changes in Prototype V2.
   6.​ Demonstrate human judgment: Clearly explain how your reasoning, decisions, and evaluation
       contributed to the outcome beyond AI-generated artifacts.

The emphasis is on requirements engineering, traceability, and evidence-based decision-making, rather
than the visual polish or technical complexity of the prototype. Remember: Your goal is not simply to
demonstrate that you can build software using AI coding assistance. Your goal is to demonstrate how and
why understanding stakeholder needs, making informed requirements decisions, and evaluating what
was built remain essential responsibilities of a software engineer.
