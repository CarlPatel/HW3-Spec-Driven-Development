Use cases

UC-1: Diagnose a press fault and capture findings

Actors: Mechanic, Maintenance system

Preconditions

  1. A press is showing a fault or producing bad parts.
  2. The mechanic uses the tablet from a safe stationary position away from moving machinery.

Flow of events

  1. UC-1@1 The mechanic selects the affected asset record.
  2. UC-1@2 The mechanic reviews the shift handoff, prior troubleshooting, closed work orders, and recorded part numbers.
  3. UC-1@3 The system displays actual temperature, setpoint, and normal range together.
  4. UC-1@4 If a live reading is unavailable or stale, the system flags that condition instead of presenting it as current; the mechanic obtains a current reading before relying on it.
  5. UC-1@5 The mechanic compares temperature behavior with part quality and heater cycling to form the diagnosis.
  6. UC-1@6 The mechanic records the asset, symptom or fault code, actual reading, and setpoint.
  7. UC-1@7 The mechanic attaches a quick photo or short voice note rather than typing the entire account.
  8. UC-1@8 If a voice note is used, the mechanic confirms that its content accurately represents the finding before publishing it.
  9. UC-1@9 The mechanic records the required shared facts separately from personal notebook content; personal notes are private unless the mechanic explicitly selects content to share.
  10. UC-1@10 The mechanic captures the finding and any change before moving on, using the briefest practical interaction.

Postconditions

  1. The shared record contains the identified fault, measured values, and captured evidence.
  2. Personal working notes have not been copied into the shared record without the mechanic selecting them.

Non-functional requirements

  1. Capture should require little effort with gloves and in noise.
  2. Tablet use must not distract personnel around moving machinery.
  3. An unavailable live reading is visibly distinguished from a current reading.

UC-2: Locate a replacement part, repair, and verify the press

Actors: Mechanic, Maintenance system

Preconditions

  1. The mechanic has identified a suspected press fault and needs a replacement part.
  2. Physical work follows the plant safety procedure; a software record is not proof of physical isolation.

Flow of events

  1. UC-2@1 The mechanic retrieves the exact part number, stock quantity, crib location, and any approved substitute.
  2. UC-2@2 The mechanic checks the crib bin against the press part number and obtains the matching part.
  3. UC-2@3 The mechanic stops the press and confirms lockout/tagout before opening equipment.
  4. UC-2@4 The mechanic records the confirmed safety check on the tablet.
  5. UC-2@5 The mechanic checks the heater band and connections, replaces the weak band, and records what was tested or changed and the installed part number.
  6. UC-2@6 The mechanic performs the verification run and records actual temperature, part quality, and whether verification passed.
  7. UC-2@7 If verification fails, the mechanic records the failure and leaves the issue open for further diagnosis; a successful repair is not declared.

Postconditions

  1. The work order records tests, corrective action, installed part, measured values, and verification status.
  2. The outcome is available for the next shift to avoid repeating undocumented checks.

Non-functional requirements

  1. Isolation must be confirmed before equipment is opened.
  2. Repair evidence must remain traceable to the equipment and work order.

UC-3: Complete a work order and acknowledge the shift handover

Actors: Mechanic, Outgoing supervisor, Incoming supervisor, Maintenance system

Preconditions

  1. A maintenance work order or unresolved machine issue must be handed to the next shift.

Flow of events

  1. UC-3@1 The outgoing supervisor reviews stopped machines, open repairs, machine status, work ownership, expected restoration, and production impact.
  2. UC-3@2 The system presents linked downtime, alarms, diagnosis, corrective action, reason code, parts, readings, and verification details.
  3. UC-3@3 The supervisor checks downtime minutes against the machine stop and first good part.
  4. UC-3@4 If the work order is being completed, the system requires a reason code; missing codes or other required fields are shown to the supervisor.
  5. UC-3@5 The supervisor obtains the missing reason before completion, and assigns responsibility for missing safety-release items.
  6. UC-3@6 The outgoing and incoming supervisors agree the open-work owner, what to monitor, and when to escalate.
  7. UC-3@7 The incoming supervisor acknowledges the digital handoff after the critical fields are complete.
  8. UC-3@8 The system associates the handover facts with the equipment, time window, and shift for access across all three shifts.

Postconditions

  1. The next shift has an acknowledged handover with explicit machine status and work ownership.
  2. A completed work order includes its reason code and checked downtime minutes; unresolved issues remain identified.

Non-functional requirements

  1. Missing required data must be visible instead of disappearing into the next shift.
  2. Maintenance data must be accurate enough to support decisions.

UC-4: Prioritize and escalate an unresolved machine issue

Actors: Shift supervisor, Maintenance lead, Production supervisor, Angela

Preconditions

  1. A machine issue remains unresolved and its production or safety impact needs review.

Flow of events

  1. UC-4@1 The supervisor checks whether the machine is a bottleneck, lacks a practical backup, stops a production cell, or has a history of long downtime or high-impact quality issues.
  2. UC-4@2 The supervisor reviews the restoration estimate, current status, and production impact.
  3. UC-4@3 If the restoration target is exceeded, critical equipment is affected, or a safety or quality risk exists, the supervisor escalates to the maintenance lead, production supervisor, or Angela.
  4. UC-4@4 The escalation record identifies its owner, time, reason, and next action.
  5. UC-4@5 The supervisor includes monitoring and escalation expectations in the handover.

Postconditions

  1. An issue meeting an escalation condition has a named escalation owner and next action.
  2. The next shift can see the current escalation state.

Non-functional requirements

  1. Priority should reflect actual equipment and production impact rather than an unclassified alarm alone.

UC-5: Review safety hold and authorize return to service

Actors: Mechanic, Shift supervisor, Authorized release person, Maintenance system

Preconditions

  1. A repaired or held machine is being considered for return to service.

Flow of events

  1. UC-5@1 The technician records the failure, corrective action, replaced parts, readings, verification outcome, and safety documentation.
  2. UC-5@2 The isolation record identifies the equipment, isolation points, each lock applier, date and time, and release authorizer and performer.
  3. UC-5@3 The supervisor checks hazard assessment, isolation, alarm status, applicable permits, inspection, and unresolved hazards and validates the controls.
  4. UC-5@4 The supervisor identifies the person responsible for each missing release item; the hold remains visible.
  5. UC-5@5 If required fields or release documentation are incomplete, or a hazard is unresolved, equipment remains on hold despite production pressure.
  6. UC-5@6 If a release request is attempted while checks are incomplete, the system rejects the request and displays the missing items.
  7. UC-5@7 If the required controls and records are complete and hazards addressed, the authorized person records the return-to-service authorization and the technician/supervisor acknowledgments before equipment release.
  8. UC-5@8 The system retains the permit, photos, alarms, release decision, and history of record changes with the work order.

Postconditions

  1. Equipment is either held with visible outstanding items or released following recorded approval and validated controls.
  2. The decision, responsible persons, and supporting records are traceable.

Non-functional requirements

  1. Production pressure must not override the safety hold.
  2. Personnel must not be penalized for following the procedure and stopping an unsafe restart.
  3. The system records and checks authorization; it does not itself apply locks or physically prove isolation.

UC-6: Report and follow through on a near miss

Actors: Reporter, Shift supervisor, Safety coordinator, Corrective-action owner

Preconditions

  1. A near miss is observed or mentioned during a handoff, even if nobody is injured.

Flow of events

  1. UC-6@1 The person hearing or observing the near miss opens a report with what happened, where, when, and who was involved, within 24 hours.
  2. UC-6@2 A named person owns the report and the supervisor acknowledges it before the 24-hour deadline.
  3. UC-6@3 The report is treated as part of normal handover and is not closed merely because the event was mentioned verbally.
  4. UC-6@4 The safety coordinator reviews the facts, existing controls, and changes needed to prevent recurrence without punishing the reporter for speaking up.
  5. UC-6@5 A named person owns any resulting corrective action, and its completion is verified.

Postconditions

  1. The near miss has a recorded, acknowledged report and reviewed facts.
  2. Any identified corrective action has an owner and verified completion before that action is treated as complete.

Non-functional requirements

  1. Reporting and supervisor acknowledgment occur within the stakeholder-specified 24 hours.
  2. Required reporting facts should be brief and review should focus on unsafe conditions rather than blame.

UC-7: Create a separate environmental spill report

Actors: Reporter, Safety coordinator

Preconditions

  1. A chemical spill occurs during maintenance.

Flow of events

  1. UC-7@1 The reporter creates a separate environmental report for the chemical spill instead of recording it only as a repair-log note.
  2. UC-7@2 The reporter references the affected equipment and maintenance time window so the spill report can be found alongside the maintenance context.

Postconditions

  1. The spill is documented in a separate environmental report.

Non-functional requirements

  1. The environmental report remains distinct from ordinary repair notes.

UC-8: Investigate a safety alarm or recurring failure

Actors: Mechanic, Shift supervisor, Safety coordinator

Preconditions

  1. A sensor alarm or recurring failure calls for a maintenance safety decision.

Flow of events

  1. UC-8@1 The supervisor stops affected work and documents the hazard if the alarm requires investigation.
  2. UC-8@2 The technician and supervisor verify the hazard, isolation, and whether the alarm is still active; the safety coordinator reviews the permit and equipment history.
  3. UC-8@3 The alarm information is preserved with the maintenance record.
  4. UC-8@4 The safety coordinator reviews recurring alarms and failure modes and determines whether shutdown, corrective action, or a formal safety inspection is needed.
  5. UC-8@5 The equipment remains unreleased until the hazard is addressed and required records and authorization are complete.

Postconditions

  1. The alarm and hazard decision are recorded and an unsafe release has been prevented.

Non-functional requirements

  1. Safety-critical findings must be prominent rather than buried in general maintenance notes.
  2. Tablet use must not distract personnel around moving machinery.

UC-9: Retrieve and export an inspectable equipment history

Actors: Safety coordinator, Inspector, Maintenance system

Preconditions

  1. An inspector requests maintenance and safety evidence for equipment and a time period.

Flow of events

  1. UC-9@1 The safety coordinator enters the equipment identifier and requested time window.
  2. UC-9@2 The system retrieves the linked maintenance, isolation, alarms, permits, photos, corrective actions, acknowledgments, and return-to-service history.
  3. UC-9@3 The coordinator reviews who changed what and when, retaining the history of corrections.
  4. UC-9@4 The coordinator retrieves readable records for the full inspection period even if equipment has been retired or personnel have changed jobs.
  5. UC-9@5 If the applicable inspection retention period has not been configured, the safety coordinator defines it before any record disposal is scheduled; no arbitrary duration is substituted.
  6. UC-9@6 The coordinator exports the requested records in a form the inspector can review without access to the entire operating system.

Postconditions

  1. The inspector can review a complete equipment history and correction history without relying on staff memory.

Non-functional requirements

  1. Records remain readable and retrievable for the full applicable inspection period.
  2. Export does not require granting broad access to the operating system.
